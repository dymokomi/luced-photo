# The Godfather (1972)

Gordon Willis rated Eastman Color Negative at 250 and had it pushed a stop, "theoretically a half-stop underexposure," lit from overhead and printed on one light for the whole film, with what he called a brassy yellow. The release prints were Technicolor dye transfer, and Sicily was shot through chocolate filters.

This look uses Eastman Color Negative 5254, half a stop under and pushed in ECN-1 (Kodak never tabled pushing it; 16 minutes is an inference), printed warm and yellow as Technicolor dye transfer, the matrices made from the cut negative. The printer lights are an inference from Willis's words, and the dye-transfer dyes and tone an estimate from measurements of a 1949 print. For the Sicily scenes, add Chocolate on the Filter stage.
