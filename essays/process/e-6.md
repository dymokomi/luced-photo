# E-6: the reversal process

E-6 makes a positive slide in one film. The first developer is a black-and-white developer: it turns exposed grains into a silver negative image and uses them up. A reversal bath then fogs every grain that was not exposed (older processes used a flash of light). The color developer develops those grains, forming dyes where the scene was dark. Bleach and fix remove all the silver, and the dyes remain as a positive.

Everything depends on the first developer, at 37.8 °C for six minutes. Developing it longer uses up more of the grains, so less dye forms and the slide gets lighter: that is how slides are pushed. Each stop of push costs some density in the blacks and shifts color slightly.

E-6 replaced the older E-4 in the late 1970s and remains the process for every slide film still made, from Velvia to Ektachrome.
