# PMK pyro

Pyrogallol was one of the first developing agents, used from the 1850s. As it develops it oxidizes into products that stain and harden the gelatin around each grain, in proportion to the silver. Gordon Hutchings revived it in the 1980s as PMK (Pyro, Metol, Kodalk), described in The Book of Pyro, and it became a favourite of large-format photographers.

The stain does two things. It masks the grain, so prints look smoother than the silver alone would. And it is yellow-green, so it adds printing density in the highlights mainly for blue-sensitive papers, compressing them. The hardened gelatin also limits the sideways diffusion of developer, which gives sharp, well-separated edges.
