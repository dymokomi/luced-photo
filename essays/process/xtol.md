# Kodak XTOL

XTOL, introduced by Kodak in 1996, replaced the hydroquinone of classic developers with ascorbate, vitamin C, and uses a phenidone-type agent, Dimezone S. It is low in toxicity and was designed to give more from the same film.

Kodak claimed that most films gain about a third of a stop of real speed in XTOL, and that prints can be ten percent larger for the same grain and sharpness. It became a favourite for modern T-grain films and for scanning. Its one well-known weakness was sudden failure: an exhausted or contaminated batch could stop developing without warning, so careful users tested each batch.
