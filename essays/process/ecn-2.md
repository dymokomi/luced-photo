# ECN-2: Eastman Color Negative, process 2

ECN-2 is the process for motion picture color negative, used since the 1970s. The film first goes through a prebath that softens the rem-jet, the black anti-halation backing, which is then washed off. Then the color developer, CD-3, works for three minutes at 41.1 °C (106 °F): where it reduces exposed silver halide, its oxidized form couples with the dye couplers in each layer, building cyan, magenta and yellow dye around each developing grain.

A stop bath ends development, a bleach turns the silver back into halide, and a fixer dissolves all the remaining halide away. What is left is the dye image and the orange mask, colored couplers that did not react and correct the dyes' unwanted absorptions.

Kodak's push times are 3:40 for one stop and 4:40 for two; a pull is 2:30. A push buys less than a third of a stop of real shadow speed for the first stop and about half a stop for two; the rest is contrast, grain, and a drift toward yellow highlights and blue shadows. The developer is held to a tenth of a degree, because a contrast mismatch between rolls would show from reel to reel.
