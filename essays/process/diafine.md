# Diafine, a two-bath developer

Diafine splits the developer in two. In the first bath the film soaks up developing agents, which cannot work without alkali; in the second, the alkali switches them on. The film develops only with what its emulsion absorbed, so where the image is dense the developer runs out, while the shadows develop fully.

That has two remarkable consequences. Time and temperature hardly matter: the maker says they have no practical effect between 21 and 29 °C. And films come out faster at a moderate, fixed contrast: the maker rates Tri-X at EI 1600, Acros at 200. Independent tests usually find about one stop rather than two for Tri-X, with highlights that never block up.
