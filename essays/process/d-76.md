# D-76

Kodak published D-76 in 1927, originally for motion picture negative film. It is a metol–hydroquinone developer with a large amount of sodium sulfite, about 100 grams per litre, which preserves it and also dissolves a little of the silver halide as it develops, softening the grain.

D-76 became the reference developer of black-and-white photography: film speeds and grain are judged against it, and almost every data sheet gives its times. It gives full film speed, moderate grain and good sharpness, a balance few developers improve on in every respect at once. Ilford's ID-11 is essentially the same formula.

Diluted 1:1 and used once, it gives slightly sharper negatives. Time and temperature go together: the Acros II data sheet gives 8½ minutes at 18 °C and 4½ at 26 °C.
