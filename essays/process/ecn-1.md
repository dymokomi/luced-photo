# ECN-1: color negative in the 1960s

Before ECN-2, Eastman Color Negative was developed in ECN-1. Its color developer, SD-30, worked slowly and cool: about twelve minutes at 21 °C, where ECN-2 takes three minutes at 41 °C. Faster, hotter processes came in the 1970s as labs needed more throughput and Kodak's emulsions changed.

This was the process of the great 65 mm productions of the 1960s, among them 2001: A Space Odyssey, shot in Super Panavision 70 on Eastman Color Negative. Kodak's 1968 paper introducing the next negative, 5254, gives the ECN-1 sequence, and states that 5254's spectral sensitivities and dyes were the same as 5251's, the negative of that era.
