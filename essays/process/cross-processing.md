# Cross-processing: slide film as a negative

Develop slide film in C-41 and there is no first developer, no reversal: the color developer forms dye wherever the film was exposed, so the slide comes out a negative. It has none of a negative's orange mask, the colored couplers that correct the dyes' unwanted absorptions, and the reversal emulsion was made to be steep. Scanned or printed, the result is very contrasty and loudly colored, with crossovers: one hue in the highlights and its complement in the shadows. Fujifilm slide films are known to go yellow-green and cyan.

It was found by accident many times, and became a look of 1990s fashion and music photography. In cinema it appears in Clockers, U-Turn, and the "Demon-Vision" sequences of Fallen; Newton Thomas Sigel used cross-processed Ektachrome on Three Kings. Labs were wary of it: without E-6's final stabilizer, the magenta dye of cross-processed Ektachrome fades.

Here the slide's curves are turned into the negative C-41 makes of them, at a lower contrast than the slide's own, over a clear base, and scanned like any negative, without the color correction a stock's own scan gets.
