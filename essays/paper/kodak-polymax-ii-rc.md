# Kodak Polymax II RC

Polymax II RC was one of Kodak's last black-and-white enlarging papers: a medium-weight, resin-coated, selective-contrast paper with a neutral-black image tone. Kodak's data sheet G-26 (June 2005) announces that Kodak would stop selling black-and-white papers by the end of that year, as printing moved to digital output.

Like other selective-contrast papers, it changes contrast with the color of the exposing light: yellow filtration favors its low-contrast, green-sensitized emulsion and magenta filtration its high-contrast, blue-sensitive one. Kodak's POLYMAX filters give twelve steps from -1 to 5+, and the sheet gives a nominal white-light grade of 2.5. Its ISO table (F surface, DEKTOL developer) runs from R140 with filter -1 to R40 with 5+, at P250 through filter 3 1/2 and P125 above, and P500 with no filter. Its spectral sensitivity curves, at densities 0.30, 0.70 and 1.60 above D-min, fall to nothing just past 550 nm, which is why it can be handled under an amber safelight.

The sheet draws four vector graphs of characteristic curves on an absolute log exposure axis in lux-seconds, each with the white-light curve and three filters, so all thirteen exposures are read here. D-max is about 2.1 and D-min about 0.06. Kodak noted its very rich blacks, and that it was designed to print most negatives without filtration at full speed.
