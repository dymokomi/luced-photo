# Kodak Polycontrast IV RC

Polycontrast IV RC was the last of Kodak's Polycontrast selective-contrast papers, a resin-coated, medium-weight black-and-white enlarging paper with a neutral-black tone. Kodak's data sheet G-4037 (revised June 2005) carries the same notice as Polymax II RC's: Kodak black-and-white papers would no longer be sold after the end of 2005.

It is exposed through the same POLYMAX filters as Polymax II RC, and its sheet prints the same ISO table figure for figure: R140 with filter -1 down to R40 with 5+ in DEKTOL, P500 with no filter, P250 through 3 1/2 and P125 for 4 to 5+. Like Polymax, it combines a blue-sensitive high-contrast emulsion with a green-sensitized low-contrast one, and its spectral sensitivity ends near 555 nm. Kodak advertised improved highlight tone reproduction and an image that is neutral but easily toned, for example with Sepia II Warm Toner.

The sheet's characteristic curves, all read here from vector graphs, show white light and filters 1, 3 and 5+ on the glossy F, lustre E and matte N surfaces, each tray processed in DEKTOL and machine processed in POLYMAX RT developer. D-max is about 2.1 on F, a little more in the machine process, and about 1.7 on the matte N surface, a reminder that a print's maximum black depends on its surface as much as its emulsion.
