# Eastman Color Negative 5251

Eastman Color Negative 5251 was Kodak's tungsten camera negative of the 1960s, introduced in 1962 to replace 5250 with finer grain and better color. It was rated EI 50 at 3200 K and was developed in the original ECN process (later called ECN-1): 12 minutes in SD-30 at 21 °C, with a ferricyanide bleach. Prints were made on Eastman Color Print 5385.

2001: A Space Odyssey was photographed in Super Panavision 70, on 65 mm negative with spherical lenses, and released in 70 mm prints from April 1968. The common technical listings name the camera stock as Eastman 5251. That fits the dates: its faster successor, 5254, was only presented to the SMPTE in May 1968, after the premiere, so 5251 is very likely, though no Kodak or production document we have names it.

No data sheet for 5251 survives online. The curves here come from Kodak's 1968 SMPTE paper on 5254, which states that the new film has the same structure, the same spectral sensitivities and the same dyes as 5251, and is one stop faster; the curves are moved one stop to account for that. The paper's exposure axis carries no numbers, so where normal exposure falls is an estimate, and it publishes no grain or sharpness data.
