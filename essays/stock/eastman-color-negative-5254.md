# Eastman Color Negative 5254

Kodak presented 5254 to the SMPTE in May 1968 as its new camera negative: the same structure, spectral sensitivities and dyes as 5251, and one stop faster, rated 100 under tungsten light. It was developed in ECN-1, and it carried American features into the early 1970s, until ECN-2 and 5247 replaced it.

It is the negative of much of the New Hollywood. Gordon Willis rated it at 250 and pushed it a stop for The Godfather, and Stanley Kubrick and John Alcott pushed the whole of Barry Lyndon a stop on it, its candlelit scenes lit by candles alone through a Zeiss f/0.7 lens.

No data sheet for 5254 survives online. Since Kodak's own paper says it is 5251 a stop faster, it is built here from 5251's curves moved one stop; its grain, coarser for the speed, is an estimate.
