# Kodak Portra 160

Portra arrived in 1998 as the successor to Kodak's Vericolor professional films, first in NC (natural color) and VC (vivid color) versions. In 2011 the two 160-speed films were replaced by a single Portra 160, made with Kodak's Vision motion picture film technology, the same step the new Portra 400 had taken a year earlier.

Kodak describes it as having finer grain for scanning and enlargement while keeping the "smooth and natural skin tone reproduction" the family is known for, and recommends it for portrait, fashion and commercial work in the studio or on location. Its contrast is low and its color restrained, so it renders faces and pastel light gently and holds detail in bright highlights.

The sheet gives normal exposure at log H -1.051 and a Print Grain Index of 28 for a 4x6 print from 35 mm, finer than Portra 400's 37. Its curves, sensitivities, dye densities and MTF are read from the sheet's vector graphs. Kodak publishes no rms granularity for it, so the grain strength used here is an estimate scaled from the Print Grain Index.
