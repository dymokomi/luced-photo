# Kodak Ektachrome E100

Kodak stopped making its Ektachrome slide films in 2012. Kodak Alaris announced their return in January 2017, and Ektachrome E100 arrived in 2018: a new daylight slide film for the E-6 process, rated ISO 100.

The sheet describes it plainly: extremely fine grain (diffuse rms 8), a low D-min for clean whites, moderately enhanced saturation with a neutral balance, and a low-contrast tone scale. It is a T-Grain emulsion and needs no correction for exposures from 1/10,000 s to 10 s. Kodak matched its three color records so that the grey scale stays neutral from highlights into shadows.

As a reversal film it is developed into a positive, and its characteristic curves fall as exposure rises, from a maximum density near 3.7 down to the clear base. Kodak's own advice is that a slide film has much less exposure range than a negative, about half a stop either way for the best result, so it rewards careful metering.
