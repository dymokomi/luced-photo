# Kodak Tri-X 400

Tri-X is one of the longest-lived films still made. Kodak introduced it in 1940 as a sheet film and brought it to 35 mm and 120 in 1954. Its current version, 400TX, is a high-speed panchromatic black-and-white negative with conventional, cubic grain, and Kodak recommends it for dim light, fast action and push processing.

Its look is the classic one of documentary and street photography: visible, crisp grain, strong midtone contrast and a shoulder that holds bright skies. It responds strongly to developer and time, which is why photographers have pushed it to EI 1600 and 3200 for decades.

Kodak's sheet draws characteristic curves for D-76 at 6, 8, 10 and 12 minutes and for T-MAX Developer at 6, 7, 9 and 11 minutes, each for 35 mm and 120. The simulator uses them to model how development changes contrast. It also gives a spectral sensitivity reaching to about 650 nm, an MTF curve, and a diffuse rms granularity of 17, about twice T-MAX 100's.
