# Fujifilm Neopan 100 Acros II

Acros was Fujifilm's fine-grained ISO 100 black-and-white film, known for some of the finest grain and best sharpness of any conventional black-and-white film, and for remarkable reciprocity: it needs no exposure correction for exposures up to two minutes, which made it a favourite for night photography and long exposures.

Fujifilm stopped making it in 2018, and Acros II appeared in 2019, made with a new emulsion technology Fujifilm calls P.I.D.C. Its data sheet gives characteristic curves for D-76 at 4, 7 and 10 minutes, which this simulator uses to model how development time changes contrast and speed, and a granularity of 7 in Microfine developer.

Acros' grain is a fine, tight texture, which is why Fujifilm's digital cameras imitate it with a grain of their own. Here its grain comes from small, crisp clouds of silver on a single layer.
