# Kodak Vision3 500T 5219

Vision3 500T was the first of the Vision3 family, released in November 2007, and it is still in production. It is a 500-speed tungsten negative for the ECN-2 process, rated EI 500 at 3200 K and 320 in daylight behind an 85 filter. Kodak kept the overall look of Vision2 and changed the two ends of the curve: dye layering technology lowers the grain in the shadows, and what Kodak calls sub-micron imaging sensors give the highlights more room. The sheet says about two extra stops of highlight detail can be recovered when the film is scanned.

Its characteristic curves span sixteen stops on Kodak's graph and are still climbing at the bright end, which is why Kodak widened the graph's exposure scale for Vision3. Normal exposure sits halfway along that scale, eight stops from either end of the graph.

Vision3 500T is also the film inside CineStill 800T, which is sold with the rem-jet backing removed so that it can be developed in C-41. Without that backing, light scattered back from the base gives bright lights their red halo.
