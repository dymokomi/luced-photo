# Fujifilm Velvia 50

When Fujifilm introduced Velvia in 1990, it changed landscape and nature photography. Its colors are far more saturated than the slide films before it, especially greens and reds, with deep blacks and very fine grain. It was rated ISO 50, and many photographers rated it at 40.

Velvia is a reversal film: it is developed in E-6 into a slide, a positive to be seen on a light table or projected. It has a narrow exposure range, about five stops, and little forgiveness for overexposure, so landscape photographers metered carefully and shot in soft light, often with graduated filters to hold the sky.

The original was discontinued in 2005 because some of its materials became unavailable, and Velvia 50 returned in 2007 in a new formulation made to look the same. The curves here come from that film's data sheet.
