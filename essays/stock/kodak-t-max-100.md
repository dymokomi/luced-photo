# Kodak T-MAX 100

Kodak introduced T-MAX 100 and T-MAX 400 in 1986, the first black-and-white films built on its tabular T-Grain emulsions. Flat silver crystals catch more light for their volume than cubic ones, so the film reaches a given speed with finer grain. Kodak's sheet describes it as having extremely high sharpness, extremely fine grain and very high resolving power, and suggests it for detailed subjects, copying and photomicrography.

It is a cleaner, more clinical film than Tri-X, with a long straight curve and smooth tones. Its developer and time matter more than with traditional films, and Kodak warns against developing it by inspection.

The sheet gives a diffuse rms granularity of 8 in D-76 and resolving power of 63 lines/mm at low contrast and 200 at high contrast. Its characteristic curves cover D-76 at 6, 7.5 and 10 minutes, T-MAX RS at four times and T-MAX Developer at four times, and its MTF stays near 100 percent to about 50 cycles/mm. All of these are read from the sheet's vector graphs.
