# Kodak Portra 400

Portra arrived in 1998 as the successor to Kodak's Vericolor professional films, first in NC (natural color) and VC (vivid color) versions. In late 2010 a single new Portra 400 replaced them, and Kodak said it was built with technology from its Vision motion picture films, aiming at natural skin tones and fine grain for its speed. It became the color negative of wedding, portrait and fashion photographers, and later of a new generation shooting film for its look.

Like all color negatives, it has wide latitude: it holds detail when overexposed by several stops, and many photographers rate it at 200 or lower for denser, cleaner negatives and softer pastel color. Its sheet calls it the world's finest-grain high-speed color negative.

Kodak's still sheets draw the midscale neutral but not each dye, so the dye shapes here are Vision3 250D's, the motion picture film Portra's technology comes from. Its characteristic curves, spectral sensitivities and MTF are read from the sheet itself; Kodak gives a print grain index rather than an rms granularity, so its grain is an estimate between Vision3 250D's and 500T's.
