# Kodak Ektar 100

Kodak introduced Ektar 100 in 2008, reviving a name it had used for a line of fine-grained films in the late 1980s. It is a daylight color negative for Process C-41, and Kodak's sheet calls it the finest-grained color negative film available, with high saturation and "ultra-vivid" color. It is built with the same Vision motion picture technology as the current Portra films, so it shares their dye set.

Ektar is known for strong, clean color and more contrast than Portra. Kodak recommends it for nature, travel and outdoor photography, and for fashion and product work. Kodak gives no rms granularity for it, only a Print Grain Index: below 25, the threshold of visible grain, for a 4x6 print from 35 mm, and 38 at 8x10.

The sheet's curves are drawn as vectors, and the simulator reads them directly: characteristic curves with normal exposure at log H -0.84, the three layers' spectral sensitivities, the midscale neutral and D-min dye densities, and MTF curves that stay near 100 percent to about 20 cycles/mm. Its grain strength here is an estimate scaled from the Print Grain Index.
