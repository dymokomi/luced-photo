# Kodak Vision 500T 5279

In 1996 Kodak began replacing EXR with Vision, and 5279 was the first: a 500-speed tungsten negative with finer grain than the EXR 500T it succeeded. Its sheet places a normal grey exposure about four stops above the film's threshold, which shows how much shadow latitude a negative carries below a correctly exposed face.

Vision 500T became one of the most used stocks of the late 1990s and early 2000s, in features, commercials and television. It was followed by Vision2 500T 5218 in 2002 and Vision3 500T 5219 in 2007, still in production today. Each generation kept the look recognizably Kodak, warm and smooth, with less grain and more highlight latitude.
