# Kodak Vision Color Print Film 2383

Kodak introduced Vision Color Print Film 2383 in 1998, and it became the release print stock of most of the films projected in the last two decades of film projection. It descends from Eastman Color Print 5385 of 1962 and its successors 5383, 5384 and 5386, each with more stable dyes and a little more density.

Print film is made to be projected: very slow, very steep, with a maximum density above 4, so a dark theater shows deep blacks. Release prints were struck at high speed from an internegative, itself printed from an interpositive made from the cut camera negative: each duplicate softened the image a little, about fifteen percent of its resolution each in a 2004 SMPTE study, and added its own grain. Answer and show prints were struck straight from the negative and were sharper.

Here Generations sets how many duplicates stood between the negative and the print, and Optical Step adds an optical printer, as Super 35 needed to squeeze its frame to anamorphic and blow-ups to 70 mm needed to enlarge it.
