# Filters on the lens

A filter changes the light before the film sees it. Two families matter here.

Conversion filters fix a mismatch between the light and the film. The orange 85 turns daylight into something close to the 3200 K that tungsten stock expects, and costs two thirds of a stop. Without it, tungsten film outdoors goes blue.

Black-and-white photographers use strong color filters to change which colors become light or dark grey. A yellow 8 darkens a blue sky a little, as the eye expects. An orange 21 darkens it more and lightens skin. A red 25 turns the sky almost black and makes clouds dramatic; Ansel Adams made Monolith, the Face of Half Dome, with a deep red filter. A green 11 lightens foliage. A blue 47 does the opposite of the red.

Every filter takes away light. The filter factor is how much more exposure it needs, and this node opens up by that factor, as the photographer would.
