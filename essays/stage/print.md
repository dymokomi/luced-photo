# Printing: the negative onto print film

In the cinema chain, the camera negative was printed onto print film: a fine-grained, high-contrast stock exposed through the negative by a printer, then developed. The print is a positive, meant to be projected.

The lab controlled color with printer lights: the red, green and blue intensities of the printer lamp, in steps called points. A timer chose them shot by shot, which is where the term color timing comes from. Raising a light adds density in that color's dye on the print.

Print film is steep: its contrast multiplies the negative's, and its dyes give the deep blacks and saturated, slightly crushed color of a film print. Kodak Vision Color Print Film 2383 was the release print stock of most films from the late 1990s on, and it is the print stock here.
