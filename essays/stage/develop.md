# Development: making the latent image visible

The developer is a reducing agent. It turns exposed grains, those carrying a latent image, into metallic silver, and leaves unexposed grains alone, at least for a while. Given enough time, it starts on them too, which shows as fog.

How far development goes depends on time, temperature and agitation. Longer, warmer or more agitated development produces more density, mostly in the highlights, so contrast rises. It also lets weakly exposed grains develop, which effectively raises the film's speed. That is a push: rate the film faster, underexpose, and develop longer. A pull does the opposite.

In color development, the developer that has given up its electrons reacts with the couplers next to the grain and forms dye. The silver is later bleached away, leaving only the dye clouds, which is why color grain looks soft and round.

Here, time and temperature start at the process's own normal, from the manufacturer's tables.
