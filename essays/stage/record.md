# Recording: the digital intermediate back onto film

From the late 1990s, films were increasingly finished digitally: the negative scanned, graded on a computer, and then written back onto film for printing. A film recorder, a Kodak Lightning or an ARRILASER, exposes intermediate film with lasers line by line, encoding each pixel as the printing density a negative of that scene would have had, so the print film downstream sees what it expects.

O Brother, Where Art Thou? (2000) was the first American feature finished entirely this way, at 2K, recorded to Kodak's 5244 intermediate film. Within a decade nearly every film went through a digital intermediate, and the photochemical print became the last step of a digital process.

Here Record takes the graded scan and hands it to Print as printing density, the way the recorder did: so a recipe can grade like a colorist and still end on a projected print.
