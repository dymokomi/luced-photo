# The lens: light falling off toward the corners

Every lens lets less light reach the corners of the frame than the center. Part of it is geometry: light reaching the edge arrives at an angle, spread over more area and seen through a narrower opening, which by itself dims it by about the fourth power of the cosine of the angle. Part of it is the lens barrel cutting off oblique light, worst at wide apertures.

Cinematographers lived with it and often liked it: the gentle darkening draws the eye to the middle of the frame. Modern raw developers usually correct it away. Here it happens to the light before the film sees it, so the darker corners also get the film's response there, a slightly thinner negative and a touch more grain, rather than a vignette painted on afterwards.
