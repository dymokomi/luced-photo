# White balance: the light on the set

Light has a color. A tungsten lamp burns at about 3200 K and gives warm, orange light; daylight is near 5500 to 6500 K and bluer. Our eyes adapt and see a white shirt as white under both. Film does not adapt: it is made for one kind of light.

Motion picture stocks come in two balances. Tungsten (T) stocks such as Vision3 500T expect 3200 K studio lamps; daylight (D) stocks expect the sun. Shooting tungsten film outdoors gives a strong blue cast unless an orange 85 filter is put on the lens, at the cost of two thirds of a stop.

Here, Temperature and Tint describe the light the scene was lit by. Left as shot, they are the camera's own estimate. With the Filter node's Balance set to Matched, the light is balanced to the stock, as a lab would; set to None, the film sees the light as it was, color cast and all.
