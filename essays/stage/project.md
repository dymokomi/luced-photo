# Projection: the print in a dark theater

A film print is made to be seen by projection. A xenon arc lamp, close to daylight in color, shines through the print and a lens throws the image onto a screen many meters away.

The dark theater matters. With no other light, the eye adapts to the screen, and the print's dense blacks look truly black. Some light always strays, from the lens, the screen and the room, and lifts the deepest shadows a little: projection flare.

Projected film has a look of its own: rich, saturated midtones, highlights that roll off rather than clip, and shadows that sink. This node shows the print as it would look on such a screen.
