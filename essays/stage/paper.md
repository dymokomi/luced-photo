# The darkroom print

For most of photography's history a picture was a print: the negative put in an enlarger, its image projected onto a sheet of paper coated with emulsion, the paper developed, fixed and washed under a red or amber safelight. The paper is the last film the light goes through, and like film it has a characteristic curve, a speed and a contrast.

Black-and-white papers come in grades, or today in one variable-contrast emulsion whose grade is chosen with yellow and magenta filters: a soft grade for a contrasty negative, a hard one for a flat negative. The enlarger matters too. A condenser enlarger shows a silver negative steeper than a diffuse light does, the Callier effect, about a grade harder; a contact print, the negative laid on the paper, has no enlarger lens to add flare. Color paper is printed through a head of yellow, magenta and cyan filters, set in CC units, to balance the color.

Here the negative is printed on the paper you choose, at its grade or filtration and exposure, and the print is seen by reflection under D50 light, its white the display's white.
